# For the best ex, eval

Run this on the edited draft before returning it. Answer every check with pass or fail. Fix
any fail, then run the whole file again. Do not return a draft with an unresolved fail.

For detect requests, check only the last section.

## Countable checks

These have numbers. Count, do not estimate.

1. Em dashes in the body: **zero**. Count them.
2. Longest and shortest sentence in each paragraph over four sentences: the range must span
   at least 20 words. If the shortest is 14 and the longest is 26, fail.
3. Sentences under 6 words in the piece: at least one per long paragraph.
4. Consecutive sentences starting with the same word: no more than two.
5. Contractions: at least one per 300 words, unless the writer demonstrably never uses them.
6. Concrete anchors, meaning a number with a unit, a date, a proper noun, a named system or
   place: at least one per 200 words.
7. Three-item parallel lists of matching grammar and similar length: zero, unless the three
   items are genuinely a set and the writer wrote them that way.
8. Section or paragraph lengths within 10 percent of each other across three or more units:
   fail. That is machine symmetry.
9. Banned words from `SKILL.md` present outside of quoted examples: zero.

## Layer one, the tells

1. Are all banned words, discourse markers, and empty phrases gone unless quoted as examples?
2. Is every binary contrast ("not X, it's Y") removed, with Y stated directly?
3. Are participial tails ("highlighting", "underscoring", "ensuring", "making it a") replaced
   with a concrete consequence or deleted?
4. Are throat-clearing openers and faux-insight setups cut, except where precedence rule 3
   applies because a real admission follows?
5. Are colon reveals, rhetorical setups, negative listings, and dramatic fragments gone?
6. Is importance puffery replaced with the plain fact?
7. Is weasel attribution either sourced by name or cut, with nothing invented to fill it?
8. Is the fake-profound kicker deleted rather than rewritten into a better metaphor?
9. Is the summary-recap ending gone, so the piece ends on a concrete point or next action?
10. Are quotes and apostrophes consistent, with no emoji headings and no decorative bold?

## Layer two, the human signal

1. Does the draft speak in first person about things the writer has actually done, rather
   than analysing from outside?
2. Is there at least one anecdote anchored in detail an outsider could not supply: an
   institution, a grade, a time, a number, a named thing, a local rule?
3. Is every such detail one the writer actually gave you? Nothing invented, nothing inferred,
   nothing filled in for plausibility. This check outranks every other check in this file.
4. Is there at least one genuine admission of uncertainty, error, or exposure, and would the
   writer wince slightly at it?
5. Is hedging preserved where it reflects real uncertainty or the writer's speech, rather
   than stripped for polish?
6. Does the piece take a position that someone could disagree with?
7. Is the writing lopsided in the right place, with the part the writer cares most about
   visibly longer and better than the rest?
8. Does at least one thread go somewhere slightly unexpected, or stay in without resolving,
   because that is characteristic of the writer?

## Format

1. Was the destination established before editing?
2. For a forum post, comment, reply, DM, or email: are there zero headers, zero bullet
   points, zero numbered lists, and zero bold labels?
3. For an essay or blog post: is prose the default, with structure used only where the length
   genuinely requires navigation?
4. For a README or documentation: is structure preserved, with layer two applied to the prose
   between it rather than to the structure?
5. For an academic or professional submission: was the writer told plainly if their venue
   forbids the first-person moves this skill relies on?

## Honesty

1. Were zero facts, numbers, sources, quotes, experiences, or opinions added that the writer
   did not supply?
2. Were unicode tricks, homoglyphs, inserted typos, and thesaurus swapping all avoided?
3. Does the output avoid claiming or implying that the result is undetectable?
4. If the writer asked for something this file refuses, were they told why rather than
   quietly given a watered-down version?

## Final read

1. Would the writer recognise this as their own voice, not a better writer's voice?
2. Read it aloud in your head. Does any sentence sound like a press release?
3. Is there a single sentence in here that only this person could have written? Name it. If
   you cannot name one, the edit has failed regardless of every other pass above.
4. Does the output include the full edited draft, a short **What changed** section, and any
   outstanding questions?

## Detect requests only

1. Is each pattern named, with the line quoted and a short fix?
2. Does the report also name what is *missing*: absent first person, unanchored examples, no
   hedging, flat rhythm?
3. Is the draft left unrewritten?
4. Is there no score, no percentage, and no claim about whether a model wrote it?
5. Was an edit offered as a next step?
